A Practical Guide to Home Composting

Composting turns kitchen scraps and yard waste into a dark, crumbly soil amendment. The basics are simple: balance "greens" (nitrogen-rich material like vegetable peels and coffee grounds) with "browns" (carbon-rich material like dry leaves and cardboard), keep the pile moist but not soggy, and turn it every week or two to add air.

A healthy pile heats up as microbes break the material down, then cools and cures into finished compost over roughly two to three months. Avoid meat, dairy, and oily foods, which attract pests and slow the process. If the pile smells sour, it is usually too wet or too dense; add browns and turn it.

Finished compost can be mixed into garden beds, used as a top dressing, or steeped into a liquid feed. Most households can divert a large share of their waste this way with a single bin.
